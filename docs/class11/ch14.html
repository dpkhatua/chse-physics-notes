Oscillations

Back and forth, forever (almost) — the physics of anything that swings or bounces.

1 Periodic Motion & SHM

Periodic motion repeats itself at equal intervals of time (period T); frequency f = 1/T. Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM) is a special periodic motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to displacement and directed toward the mean position.

📐 SHM equations
F = −kx (restoring force)
x(t) = A sin(ωt + φ)
ω = 2π/T = 2πf

2 Spring–Mass System

🖊️ A block on a spring — the restoring force always pulls it back toward the centre.
🧮 Derivation: Time period of a spring-mass system
Given / Setup: A block of mass m attached to a spring of force constant k, displaced by x from equilibrium.
  1. Restoring force F = −kx (Hooke's law)
  2. By Newton's second law: ma = −kx → a = −(k/m)x
  3. Comparing with SHM equation a = −ω²x: ω² = k/m → ω = √(k/m)
  4. T = 2π/ω
✅ T = 2π√(m/k)
📐 Spring oscillator
T = 2π√(m/k)
Energy in SHM: E = ½kA² (total, constant throughout motion)
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A 0.5 kg mass attached to a spring of force constant 200 N/m performs SHM. Find its time period.
  1. T = 2π√(m/k) = 2π√(0.5/200) = 2π√(0.0025) = 2π(0.05)
🎯 Answer: T ≈ 0.314 s

3 Simple Pendulum

🖊️ For small angles, a simple pendulum's motion is SHM about the vertical.
🧮 Derivation: Time period of a simple pendulum
Given / Setup: A bob of mass m on a string of length L, displaced through a small angle θ.
  1. Restoring force along the arc = −mg sinθ ≈ −mgθ (small angle approximation, sinθ ≈ θ)
  2. θ = x/L, so restoring force = −(mg/L)x, giving acceleration a = −(g/L)x
  3. Comparing with a = −ω²x: ω² = g/L → ω = √(g/L)
✅ T = 2π√(L/g)
📐 Simple pendulum (small angle θ)
T = 2π√(L/g)
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the length of a simple pendulum whose time period is 2 s. (g = 9.8 m/s²)
  1. T = 2π√(L/g) → L = gT²/4π²
  2. L = 9.8 × 4 / (4×9.87) = 39.2/39.48
🎯 Answer: L ≈ 0.993 m (≈ 1 m — this is the 'seconds pendulum')
🧠 Yaad Rakho: Pendulum time period does not depend on the mass of the bob or the amplitude (for small angles) — only on length L and gravity g. This is exactly what's used in the CHSE lab experiment with L~T² graphs!

⚡ Quick Recap

  • SHM: restoring force F = −kx, directed toward mean position.
  • x(t) = A sin(ωt+φ); ω = 2π/T.
  • Spring: T = 2π√(m/k); total energy E = ½kA² stays constant.
  • Simple pendulum: T = 2π√(L/g) — independent of mass and (small) amplitude.
