Laws of Motion

Newton's three rules that quietly run the entire universe.

1 Newton's Three Laws

  1. First law (Inertia): A body stays at rest or in uniform motion unless acted on by a net external force. Force is not needed to keep something moving — only to change its motion.
  2. Second law: F = ma (rate of change of momentum ∝ applied force, in the direction of the force).
  3. Third law: For every action there is an equal and opposite reaction — forces always come in pairs acting on different bodies.
📐 Newton's second law (general form)
F = dp/dt
For constant mass: F = ma

Impulse = change in momentum = F × Δt (useful when force acts for a very short time, e.g. a bat hitting a ball).

Conservation of linear momentum: if no external force acts on a system, total momentum stays constant — this is why rockets, recoiling guns and collisions can all be analysed without knowing the internal forces.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. A bat exerts an average force of 200 N on a ball for 0.01 s. Find the impulse and the change in momentum.
  1. Impulse = F × Δt = 200 × 0.01 = 2 N·s
  2. By the impulse-momentum theorem, change in momentum = impulse
🎯 Answer: Impulse = change in momentum = 2 kg·m/s
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Newton's third law pairs act on DIFFERENT bodies and can never cancel each other in an FBD of a single body — a very common conceptual error in CHSE answers.

2 Friction

Friction opposes relative sliding between two surfaces. Static friction acts before motion starts and adjusts itself up to a maximum (limiting friction); kinetic friction acts once sliding begins and is roughly constant.

📐 Friction
f ≤ μsN (static)
f = μkN (kinetic)
μk < μs — always easier to keep something moving than to start it
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A block of mass 5 kg rests on a horizontal surface (μs = 0.4). Find the minimum horizontal force needed to just move it (g = 10 m/s²).
  1. Normal reaction N = mg = 5 × 10 = 50 N
  2. Limiting friction fs(max) = μsN = 0.4 × 50 = 20 N
  3. To just start moving, applied force must equal limiting friction
🎯 Answer: 20 N

3 Circular Dynamics — Banking of Roads

🖊️ Forces on a block on an inclined surface: weight mg splits into components along and perpendicular to the incline.

On a banked road, the horizontal component of the normal reaction supplies part (or all) of the required centripetal force, letting vehicles turn safely even without relying only on friction.

🧮 Derivation: Maximum safe speed on a banked road (with friction)
Given / Setup: A vehicle of mass m moves on a road banked at angle θ, radius r, coefficient of friction μ.
  1. Resolve N and friction f along horizontal (centripetal) and vertical directions.
  2. Vertical equilibrium: N cosθ = mg + f sinθ
  3. Horizontal (centripetal): N sinθ + f cosθ = mv²/r
  4. Using f = μN and solving the two equations simultaneously for v²
✅ v²max = rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μ tanθ)
📐 Maximum safe speed on a banked curve (angle θ, friction μ)
v²max = rg(tanθ + μ)/(1 − μ tanθ)

⚡ Quick Recap

  • 1st law → inertia; 2nd law → F=ma; 3rd law → action-reaction pairs on different bodies.
  • Linear momentum is conserved when no external force acts — key for collisions.
  • Static friction adjusts up to μsN; kinetic friction is roughly constant at μkN.
  • Banking of roads uses the normal reaction's horizontal component to help provide centripetal force.

  1. State Newton's first law of motion. What is another name for this law?
