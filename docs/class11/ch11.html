Thermal Properties of Matter

Heat isn't temperature — and knowing the difference solves half these problems.

1 Temperature & Thermal Expansion

🖊️ Heating a solid makes it expand — atoms vibrate more and push each other apart.

Heat is energy transferred due to a temperature difference; temperature is how hot/cold something is (average kinetic energy of molecules). Most substances expand when heated — solids in length/area/volume, liquids and gases in volume.

📐 Thermal expansion
Linear: L = L0(1 + αΔT)
Area: A = A0(1 + βΔT), β ≈ 2α
Volume: V = V0(1 + γΔT), γ ≈ 3α
Anomalous behaviour of waterWater contracts (not expands) between 0°C and 4°C, reaching maximum density at 4°C — this is why ice floats and lakes don't freeze solid from the bottom up!
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A steel rod of length 2 m at 20°C is heated to 70°C. Find its new length. (α = 1.2×10⁻⁵ /°C)
  1. ΔT = 70 − 20 = 50°C
  2. ΔL = L0αΔT = 2 × 1.2×10⁻⁵ × 50 = 1.2×10⁻³ m
  3. New length = 2 + 0.0012 = 2.0012 m
🎯 Answer: 2.0012 m

2 Specific Heat Capacity & Calorimetry

📐 Heat, specific heat & latent heat
Q = mcΔT (c = specific heat capacity)
Q = mL (L = latent heat, during change of state — temperature doesn't change!)

CP (specific heat at constant pressure) is greater than CV (at constant volume) for gases, because at constant pressure some heat also goes into doing work as the gas expands.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the heat required to convert 2 kg of ice at 0°C completely to water at 0°C. (Latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.36×10⁵ J/kg)
  1. Q = mL = 2 × 3.36×10⁵
🎯 Answer: Q = 6.72×10⁵ J

3 Heat Transfer

  1. Conduction: heat flows through a medium without the medium itself moving (touching a hot spoon).
  2. Convection: heat carried by actual movement of fluid particles (boiling water currents).
  3. Radiation: heat transferred via electromagnetic waves, needs no medium (sunlight reaching Earth).
📐 Stefan's law & Wien's law
Stefan's law: E = σT⁴ (energy radiated per unit area per unit time)
Wien's displacement law: λm T = constant

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Heat = energy in transit; temperature = degree of hotness — don't confuse the two.
  • Water's anomalous expansion (0–4°C) is why ice floats.
  • Q = mcΔT for temperature change; Q = mL for change of state at constant temperature.
  • Conduction (medium, no bulk motion), convection (bulk fluid motion), radiation (no medium needed).
