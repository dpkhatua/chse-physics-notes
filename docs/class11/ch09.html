Class XI · Unit VII · Chapter 9

Mechanical Properties of Solids

Push, pull or twist a solid — it always tries to spring back (until it can't).

1 Stress, Strain & Hooke's Law

Stress = restoring force per unit area (how hard you're pushing/pulling per unit cross-section). Strain = fractional change in dimension (deformation caused). Hooke's law: for small deformations, stress ∝ strain.

📐 Types of modulus of elasticity
Young's modulus: Y = (F/A)/(ΔL/L) — for length change
Bulk modulus: K = −ΔP/(ΔV/V) — for volume change
Shear (rigidity) modulus: G = shear stress/shear strain
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A wire of length 2 m and cross-sectional area 1 mm² is stretched by 0.5 mm under a load. If Y = 2×10¹¹ Pa, find the applied force.
  1. Y = (F/A)/(ΔL/L) → F = Y·A·ΔL/L
  2. A = 1×10⁻⁶ m², ΔL = 0.5×10⁻³ m, L = 2 m
  3. F = 2×10¹¹ × 1×10⁻⁶ × 0.5×10⁻³ / 2 = 50 N
🎯 Answer: F = 50 N

2 Stress–Strain Graph

🖊️ Beyond the elastic limit, the material doesn't return to its original shape.

Up to the elastic limit, the material returns to its original shape when the load is removed (Hooke's law region is linear). Beyond it, permanent deformation sets in, and eventually the material reaches its breaking point.

Poisson's ratio = (lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain) — how much a wire thins when stretched.

📐 Elastic potential energy stored in a stretched wire
U = ½ × stress × strain × volume = ½ × F × ΔL
💡 Score booster: For 'derive elastic PE' questions, sketch the F–extension graph and use area = ½FΔx — examiners look specifically for this graphical justification.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Stress = force/area, strain = fractional deformation; Hooke's law: stress ∝ strain (elastic limit).
  • Y for length change, K for volume change, G for shape (shear) change.
  • Beyond the elastic limit, deformation becomes permanent.
