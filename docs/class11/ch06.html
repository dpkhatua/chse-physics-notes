Work, Energy and Power

Nothing gets done in physics without work — literally.

1 Work and the Work–Energy Theorem

Work is done only when a force causes displacement in its own direction: W = F·s·cosθ. Carrying a heavy bag while walking horizontally? Physics says the vertical force (your muscles) does zero work on the bag since displacement is horizontal!

🖊️ For a variable force, work done = area under the Force–displacement graph.
🧮 Derivation: Work–energy theorem
Given / Setup: A constant force F acts on a mass m, accelerating it from u to v over displacement s.
  1. From v² = u² + 2as: a = (v²−u²)/2s
  2. Work done W = F·s = (ma)·s = m × (v²−u²)/2s × s = ½m(v²−u²)
  3. W = ½mv² − ½mu² = final KE − initial KE
✅ Wnet = ΔKE (work-energy theorem)
📐 Work–Energy theorem
Wnet = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu²
Kinetic Energy: KE = ½mv²
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A 2 kg block moving at 3 m/s is acted on by a force that brings it to rest over 1.5 m. Find the average force (assume it opposes motion).
  1. Initial KE = ½(2)(3²) = 9 J, Final KE = 0
  2. By work-energy theorem, work done by force = ΔKE = 0 − 9 = −9 J
  3. W = −F×s (force opposes motion) → −9 = −F×1.5 → F = 6 N
🎯 Answer: F = 6 N

2 Potential Energy & Conservative Forces

A force is conservative if the work it does depends only on start and end points, not the path taken (gravity, spring force). For such forces we can define a potential energy. Friction is non-conservative — it wastes energy as heat and depends on the path.

📐 Common potential energies
Gravitational PE: U = mgh
Spring PE: U = ½kx²
🧠 Yaad Rakho: Total mechanical energy (KE + PE) is conserved only when non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance) do no work.

3 Collisions

In elastic collisions, both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved (like ideal billiard balls). In inelastic collisions, momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not — some energy converts to heat/sound/deformation. A perfectly inelastic collision is the extreme case: the bodies stick together afterward.

📐 Power
Power = Work done / time = W/t
Instantaneous power: P = F·v
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A ball of mass 1 kg moving at 4 m/s collides head-on with a stationary ball of mass 3 kg and they stick together. Find their common velocity.
  1. By conservation of momentum: m1u1 + m2u2 = (m1+m2)v
  2. (1)(4) + (3)(0) = (1+3)v
  3. 4 = 4v → v = 1 m/s
🎯 Answer: v = 1 m/s (in the direction of the first ball's initial motion)
⚠️ Don't lose marks: In inelastic collisions, students often wrongly try to conserve kinetic energy too — only momentum is conserved unless the collision is explicitly stated as elastic.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • W = F s cosθ; only the force-component along displacement does work.
  • Work–energy theorem: net work done = change in kinetic energy.
  • Conservative forces (gravity, spring) → definable PE; friction is non-conservative.
  • Elastic collisions conserve both momentum & KE; inelastic collisions conserve only momentum.

Grouped the way CHSE papers weight questions. These are practice questions written in the board's style to test this chapter — not lifted from any specific year's paper.

  1. When is the work done by a force zero even though displacement is non-zero?
  2. Define power and give its SI unit.

  1. Distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions.

  1. Derive the work-energy theorem for a constant force.
