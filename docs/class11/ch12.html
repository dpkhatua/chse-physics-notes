Class XI · Unit VIII · Chapter 12

Thermodynamics

The universe's strictest accountant: energy in, energy out, nothing lost.

1 Zeroth & First Law

Zeroth law: if A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in equilibrium with C — this is what makes a thermometer meaningful.

📐 First law of thermodynamics
ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW
(Heat supplied = change in internal energy + work done by the system)
✏️ Solved Example
Q. 150 J of heat is supplied to a gas which does 60 J of work while expanding. Find the change in its internal energy.
  1. ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW
  2. 150 = ΔU + 60
  3. ΔU = 90 J
🎯 Answer: ΔU = 90 J (increase)

2 Thermodynamic Processes

🖊️ A cyclic process on a P–V diagram — the enclosed area equals net work done per cycle.
  • Isothermal: temperature constant (PV = constant); happens slowly, in good thermal contact with surroundings.
  • Adiabatic: no heat exchange with surroundings (ΔQ = 0); happens fast, or with perfect insulation.
  • Isochoric: volume constant, so no work is done (ΔW = 0).
  • Cyclic: system returns to its initial state, so ΔU = 0 over the full cycle.
📐 Work done in special processes
Isothermal: W = nRT ln(V2/V1)
Adiabatic: PVᵞ = constant (γ = Cp/Cv)
Cyclic process: Wnet = area enclosed by the P-V loop

3 Second Law of Thermodynamics

Heat doesn't flow spontaneously from a cold body to a hot one, and no engine can be 100% efficient — some heat must be rejected to a sink. This law explains why processes have a preferred direction (entropy of an isolated system never decreases).

🧠 Yaad Rakho: First law = bookkeeping (energy is conserved). Second law = direction (which way things naturally go).

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Zeroth law defines thermal equilibrium and justifies using a thermometer.
  • First law: ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW — energy conservation for thermodynamic systems.
  • Isothermal (T const), adiabatic (Q=0), isochoric (W=0), cyclic (ΔU=0 overall).
  • Second law: heat flows hot→cold spontaneously; no engine is 100% efficient.
