Waves

Energy travels, matter doesn't — the one-line summary of every wave.

1 Transverse & Longitudinal Waves

🖊️ Transverse wave: particles vibrate perpendicular to the direction of wave travel.
🖊️ Longitudinal wave: particles vibrate along the direction of travel — compressions & rarefactions.
📐 Wave basics
v = f λ (speed = frequency × wavelength)
y(x,t) = A sin(kx − ωt) — a progressive wave
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A wave has frequency 500 Hz and wavelength 0.6 m. Find its speed.
  1. v = fλ = 500 × 0.6
🎯 Answer: v = 300 m/s

2 Superposition & Standing Waves

The principle of superposition: when two or more waves overlap, the resultant displacement is the (vector) sum of individual displacements. When two identical waves travel in opposite directions (e.g. reflecting off a fixed end), they form a standing wave.

🖊️ Standing wave: fixed nodes (no motion) and antinodes (maximum motion) appear at fixed points.
📐 Strings & organ pipes (fundamental + harmonics)
Stretched string, both ends fixed: fn = n v / 2L (n = 1,2,3…)
Closed organ pipe: only odd harmonics
Open organ pipe: all harmonics
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A string of length 1 m fixed at both ends vibrates in its fundamental mode. If the wave speed on the string is 200 m/s, find the fundamental frequency.
  1. f1 = v/2L = 200/(2×1)
🎯 Answer: f1 = 100 Hz

3 Beats

When two waves of slightly different frequencies overlap, the resultant amplitude rises and falls periodically — this is heard as beats, useful for tuning musical instruments.

📐 Beat frequency
fbeat = |f1 − f2|
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Beat frequency is the DIFFERENCE of the two frequencies, not their sum or average — a very common slip under exam pressure.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Transverse: particle motion ⊥ wave direction; Longitudinal: particle motion ∥ wave direction.
  • v = fλ links speed, frequency and wavelength for every wave.
  • Standing waves = superposition of identical waves travelling in opposite directions — nodes & antinodes.
  • Beat frequency = |f1 − f2|, from superposition of two close frequencies.
