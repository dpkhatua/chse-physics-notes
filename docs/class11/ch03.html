Motion in a Straight Line

The simplest motion there is — but where every graph trick begins.

1 Describing Motion

Distance is the total path length covered (always positive, scalar). Displacement is the shortest straight-line change in position, with direction (vector). A student walking 5 m east then 5 m west covers a distance of 10 m but a displacement of zero!

Average speed = total distance / total time. Average velocity = displacement / time. Instantaneous velocity is the velocity at one instant: v = dx/dt (the slope of the position-time graph at that point).

⚠️ Don't lose marks: Students often use 'speed' and 'velocity' interchangeably in derivations — CHSE examiners specifically check whether you used the vector (velocity) correctly in problems involving direction/sign.

2 Graphs of Motion

🖊️ Position–time graph for uniformly accelerated motion — slope keeps increasing.

On a position–time graph, the slope gives velocity. A straight line = uniform velocity; a curving line = changing velocity (acceleration). On a velocity–time graph, the slope gives acceleration, and the area under the curve gives displacement.

3 Equations of Uniformly Accelerated Motion

🧮 Derivation: v = u + at (from a v-t graph)
Given / Setup: A body has initial velocity u at t = 0, and moves with constant acceleration a. Let its velocity be v at time t.
  1. By definition, acceleration a = (change in velocity)/(time taken) = (v − u)/t
  2. Rearranging: v − u = at
✅ v = u + at
🧮 Derivation: s = ut + ½at² (from a v-t graph)
Given / Setup: Same v-t graph: a straight line starting at u, rising to v after time t.
  1. Displacement = area under the v-t graph = area of rectangle (u × t) + area of triangle (½ × t × (v−u))
  2. Since (v−u) = at (from the first equation), triangle's area = ½ × t × at = ½at²
  3. Total displacement s = ut + ½at²
✅ s = ut + ½at²
📐 The three kinematics equations (constant a)
v = u + at
s = ut + ½at²
v² = u² + 2as
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A car accelerates uniformly from 18 km/h to 36 km/h in 5 s. Find (a) the acceleration (b) distance covered.
  1. Convert: u = 18 km/h = 5 m/s, v = 36 km/h = 10 m/s, t = 5 s
  2. (a) a = (v−u)/t = (10−5)/5 = 1 m/s²
  3. (b) s = ut + ½at² = (5×5) + ½(1)(5²) = 25 + 12.5 = 37.5 m
🎯 Answer: a = 1 m/s², s = 37.5 m
🧠 Yaad Rakho: 'Uvast' — u, v, a, s, t — the five characters in every kinematics problem. Write down what's given, what's asked, then pick the equation missing the unwanted letter.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Distance ≠ displacement; speed ≠ velocity — one has direction, the other doesn't.
  • Slope of x-t graph = velocity; slope of v-t graph = acceleration; area under v-t graph = displacement.
  • v = u+at, s = ut+½at², v² = u²+2as — valid only for constant acceleration.

🎯 Exam Corner — practice in CHSE question style

Grouped the way CHSE papers weight questions. These are practice questions written in the board's style to test this chapter — not lifted from any specific year's paper.

1 mark
  1. Can a body have zero velocity and non-zero acceleration at the same instant? Give an example.
  2. What does the slope of a position-time graph represent?
2 marks
  1. Derive v = u + at using a velocity-time graph.
  2. A ball is thrown vertically upward with speed 20 m/s. Find the time taken to reach the highest point (g = 10 m/s²).
3 marks
  1. Derive s = ut + ½at² using a velocity-time graph, with a clear labelled graph.
5 marks
  1. Derive all three equations of motion (v=u+at, s=ut+½at², v²=u²+2as) using calculus/graphical method, and solve: a train starting from rest accelerates at 2 m/s² for 10 s, then moves at constant velocity for 20 s. Find total distance covered.
