Motion in a Plane

Motion gets a second dimension — enter vectors, projectiles and circles.

1 Scalars, Vectors & Vector Addition

A scalar has only magnitude (mass, speed, temperature). A vector has magnitude AND direction (displacement, velocity, force). Vectors are added using the triangle law or parallelogram law — you can't just add magnitudes unless they're in the same direction!

🖊️ Triangle law: place vector B at the tip of A, the resultant R runs from A's tail to B's tip.
📐 Resolving a vector into components
Ax = A cos θ (x-component)
Ay = A sin θ (y-component)
A = √(Ax² + Ay²) , θ = tan⁻¹(Ay/Ax)
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Two forces of 6 N and 8 N act at right angles to each other. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.
  1. Since they're perpendicular, R = √(6² + 8²) = √(36+64) = √100 = 10 N
  2. Direction: tanθ = 8/6 = 1.33 → θ = tan⁻¹(1.33) ≈ 53° from the 6 N force
🎯 Answer: R = 10 N, at 53° from the 6 N force

2 Projectile Motion

Throw something at an angle and it follows a parabolic path — this is projectile motion. The trick: split it into two independent motions — horizontal (constant velocity, no acceleration) and vertical (uniform acceleration = −g).

🖊️ Horizontal velocity stays constant; gravity curves the path into a parabola.
🧮 Derivation: Range of a projectile
Given / Setup: A body is projected with speed u at angle θ to the horizontal.
  1. Time of flight T = 2u sinθ / g (time for vertical displacement to return to zero)
  2. Horizontal range R = horizontal velocity × time of flight = (u cosθ)(2u sinθ/g)
  3. R = (2u² sinθ cosθ)/g = u² sin2θ / g (using 2sinθcosθ = sin2θ)
✅ R = u² sin2θ / g — maximum when θ = 45° (sin2θ = 1)
📐 Key projectile results (launch angle θ, speed u)
Time of flight: T = 2u sinθ / g
Maximum height: H = u² sin²θ / 2g
Range: R = u² sin2θ / g
Range is maximum when θ = 45°
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A ball is projected at 30° with the horizontal at 20 m/s. Find the range and maximum height (g = 10 m/s²).
  1. R = u²sin2θ/g = (20²)(sin60°)/10 = (400)(0.866)/10 = 34.6 m
  2. H = u²sin²θ/2g = (400)(0.25)/20 = 5 m
🎯 Answer: R ≈ 34.6 m, H = 5 m
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Forgetting that horizontal velocity (u cosθ) stays CONSTANT throughout the flight — many students wrongly apply g to the horizontal component too.

3 Uniform Circular Motion

Moving in a circle at constant speed still means accelerating — because direction keeps changing! This acceleration points toward the centre: centripetal acceleration.

🧮 Derivation: Centripetal acceleration
Given / Setup: A particle moves in a circle of radius r with constant speed v.
  1. In a small time Δt, velocity direction changes by a small angle Δθ, where Δθ = Δs/r = vΔt/r.
  2. The change in velocity vector Δv has magnitude vΔθ (for small angles), directed toward the centre.
  3. Acceleration a = Δv/Δt = v(Δθ)/Δt = v × (v/r) = v²/r
✅ a = v²/r, directed toward the centre
📐 Circular motion
Centripetal acceleration: a = v²/r = ω²r
ω (angular velocity) = v/r = 2π/T
Common confusionCentripetal force is NOT a new/separate force — it's just the name for whichever real force (tension, gravity, friction, normal force) happens to point toward the centre.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Vectors add tip-to-tail (triangle law); resolve into perpendicular components to solve problems easily.
  • Projectile motion = constant-velocity horizontal + uniformly accelerated vertical motion, independently.
  • Range is max at 45°; time of flight depends only on the vertical component of velocity.
  • Uniform circular motion has constant speed but changing velocity → centripetal acceleration v²/r toward the centre.

🎯 Exam Corner — practice in CHSE question style

Grouped the way CHSE papers weight questions. These are practice questions written in the board's style to test this chapter — not lifted from any specific year's paper.

1 mark
  1. What is the angle between velocity and acceleration at the topmost point of a projectile's path?
  2. Define angular velocity and give its SI unit.
2 marks
  1. State the parallelogram law of vector addition and write the formula for the magnitude of the resultant.
  2. A stone tied to a string of length 1 m is whirled in a circle at 2 revolutions per second. Find its centripetal acceleration.
3 marks
  1. Derive the expression for maximum height and time of flight of a projectile projected at angle θ with speed u.
5 marks
  1. Derive the expression for the range of a projectile and show that the range is maximum at 45°. A cricket ball is hit at 25 m/s at 37° above horizontal — find its range, time of flight and maximum height (g=10 m/s², sin37°=0.6, cos37°=0.8).
