Mechanical Properties of Fluids

Liquids that push equally in all directions, and pipes where speed and pressure trade places.

1 Pressure & Pascal's Law

🖊️ Pascal's law: a small force on a narrow piston creates a large force on a wide piston — hydraulic lift.
📐 Fluid pressure
P = F/A
Pressure due to fluid column: P = hρg

Pascal's law: pressure applied at any point of an enclosed, incompressible fluid is transmitted equally in all directions — the basis of hydraulic lifts and hydraulic brakes.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. In a hydraulic lift, the small piston has area 5 cm² and the large piston has area 100 cm². Find the force needed on the small piston to lift a 2000 N load.
  1. Pascal's law: F1/A1 = F2/A2
  2. F1 = F2 × A1/A2 = 2000 × 5/100
🎯 Answer: F1 = 100 N

2 Viscosity & Bernoulli's Theorem

Viscosity is a fluid's internal friction — resistance to relative flow between its layers. A ball falling through a viscous fluid reaches terminal velocity when viscous drag + buoyancy balance gravity (Stokes' law).

🖊️ Where a pipe narrows, fluid speeds up and pressure drops — Bernoulli's principle.
🧮 Derivation: Bernoulli's theorem (energy conservation for flowing fluid)
Given / Setup: Consider an incompressible, non-viscous fluid flowing steadily through a pipe of varying cross-section.
  1. Applying the work-energy theorem to a fluid element: work done by pressure forces = change in (KE + PE) of the fluid element.
  2. W = (P1 − P2)ΔV, and ΔKE = ½Δm(v2² − v1²), ΔPE = Δmg(h2 − h1)
  3. Equating and dividing by ΔV (using Δm = ρΔV): P1 + ½ρv1² + ρgh1 = P2 + ½ρv2² + ρgh2
✅ P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant along a streamline
📐 Bernoulli's theorem (along a streamline, no viscosity)
P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Bernoulli's theorem applies only to non-viscous, incompressible, steady (streamline) flow — stating this assumption explicitly earns marks in CHSE answers.

3 Surface Tension

Surface tension makes a liquid surface behave like a stretched elastic membrane, minimising its area — that's why drops are spherical and insects can walk on water. It causes capillary rise in thin tubes, depending on the angle of contact between liquid and solid.

Real-life linkDetergents reduce water's surface tension so it can spread and clean into fabric fibres instead of forming beads.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Pascal's law → pressure transmitted equally in enclosed fluid → hydraulic machines.
  • Viscosity opposes relative flow; terminal velocity balances gravity, buoyancy and drag.
  • Bernoulli: P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant → speeds up where it narrows, pressure drops.
  • Surface tension minimises surface area → spherical drops, capillary rise.
