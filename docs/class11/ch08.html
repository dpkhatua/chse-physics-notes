Class XI · Unit VI · Chapter 8

Gravitation

The force that keeps your feet on the ground and planets in orbit — same law, one equation.

1 Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion

  1. Law of orbits: every planet moves in an elliptical orbit with the Sun at one focus.
  2. Law of areas: the line joining planet to Sun sweeps equal areas in equal times (planet moves faster near the Sun).
  3. Law of periods: T² ∝ r³ (square of time period ∝ cube of the semi-major axis).

2 Universal Law of Gravitation

🖊️ A satellite in circular orbit — gravity itself supplies the centripetal force.
📐 Newton's law of gravitation
F = G m1m2 / r²
G = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²

This single equation explains why apples fall AND why the Moon stays in orbit — Newton's big insight was that it's the same force at every scale.

3 g, Potential Energy, Escape Speed

📐 Variation of g
With altitude h: g' = g(1 − 2h/R) (h ≪ R)
With depth d: g' = g(1 − d/R)
🧮 Derivation: Escape speed
Given / Setup: Minimum speed ve needed for a body of mass m to just escape Earth's gravitational field from the surface (radius R, mass M), i.e. total energy = 0.
  1. Total energy at surface = KE + PE = ½mve² + (−GMm/R)
  2. For the body to just escape (reach infinity with zero velocity), total energy = 0
  3. ½mve² − GMm/R = 0 → ve² = 2GM/R
✅ ve = √(2GM/R)
📐 Energy & speeds
Gravitational PE: U = −GMm/r
Escape speed: ve = √(2GM/R)
Orbital speed (near surface): vo = √(GM/R) = ve/√2
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the escape speed from Earth's surface. (G=6.67×10⁻¹¹, M=6×10²⁴ kg, R=6.4×10⁶ m)
  1. ve = √(2GM/R) = √(2 × 6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 6×10²⁴ / 6.4×10⁶)
  2. = √(8.0×10¹⁴/6.4×10⁶) = √(1.25×10⁸)
🎯 Answer: ve ≈ 11.2 km/s
🧠 Yaad Rakho: g decreases whether you go up (altitude) or down (depth) from Earth's surface — it's maximum only right at the surface.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Kepler: elliptical orbits, equal areas in equal times, T² ∝ r³.
  • F = Gm1m2/r² — same law explains falling apples and orbiting moons.
  • g decreases with both altitude and depth from Earth's surface.
  • Escape speed ve = √(2GM/R); orbital speed vo = ve/√2.
