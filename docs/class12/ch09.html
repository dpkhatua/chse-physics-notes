Class XII · Unit VI · Chapter 9

Ray Optics and Optical Instruments

Light drawn as straight-line rays — enough to design every lens and mirror ever made.

1 Reflection, Refraction & Total Internal Reflection

📐 Mirror & refraction formulas
Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
Snell's law: n1 sinθ1 = n2 sinθ2
🖊️ Beyond the critical angle, light reflects entirely back into the denser medium.

Total internal reflection happens when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle — the basis of optical fibres carrying data as light pulses.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. The critical angle for a glass-air interface is 42°. Find the refractive index of glass.
  1. sinθc = 1/n → n = 1/sin42° = 1/0.669
🎯 Answer: n ≈ 1.49

2 Lenses

🖊️ A convex lens converges parallel rays to a focus, forming a real, inverted image here.
🧮 Derivation: Mirror formula (concave mirror, real image)
Given / Setup: An object is placed beyond the centre of curvature of a concave mirror of focal length f; using similar triangles from the ray diagram.
  1. From similar triangles (object/image height vs. their distances from the pole): comparing triangles formed by the incident ray through the centre of curvature and the mirror,
  2. Using sign convention and simplifying the geometric relation between object distance u, image distance v and focal length f
✅ 1/v + 1/u = 1/f
📐 Lens formulas
Thin lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
Lens maker's formula: 1/f = (n−1)(1/R1 − 1/R2)
Power of a lens: P = 1/f (f in metres, P in dioptre)
Combination of thin lenses in contact: 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2 + …
✏️ Solved Example
Q. An object is placed 30 cm in front of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the image distance and magnification.
  1. 1/v − 1/u = 1/f → 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/10 + 1/(−30) [taking u = −30 cm by sign convention]
  2. 1/v = 3/30 − 1/30 = 2/30 → v = 15 cm
  3. Magnification m = v/u = 15/(−30) = −0.5
🎯 Answer: v = 15 cm (real image), m = −0.5 (inverted, diminished)
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Sign convention errors (not treating distances against incident light as negative) are the #1 cause of wrong numerical answers in ray optics — always draw the ray diagram first.

3 Optical Instruments

🖊️ A concave mirror also converges rays — used in telescopes and torches.

Microscopes use two converging lenses to magnify nearby small objects. Astronomical telescopes use an objective (large aperture, collects light from far away) and an eyepiece to magnify distant objects — reflecting telescopes use a concave mirror instead of an objective lens, avoiding chromatic aberration.

📐 Magnifying power of astronomical telescope (normal adjustment)
M = fo/fe (fo, fe = focal lengths of objective, eyepiece)

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Mirror formula 1/v+1/u=1/f; Snell's law n1sinθ1 = n2sinθ2.
  • TIR happens beyond the critical angle, going denser→rarer — basis of optical fibres.
  • Lens maker's formula gives f from the lens's curvature and refractive index; P = 1/f.
  • Telescopes/microscopes combine objective + eyepiece lenses (or mirrors) for magnification.
