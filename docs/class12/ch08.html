Electromagnetic Waves

Light, radio, X-rays and gamma rays — all one family, just different wavelengths.

1 Displacement Current & the Need for it

Maxwell noticed Ampere's law alone gave inconsistent results for circuits with a capacitor (current seems to 'break' between the plates). He fixed this by proposing a displacement current — arising from a changing electric field — which exists even where no charge physically flows.

📐 Displacement current
Id = ε0 (dΦE/dt)

2 Properties of Electromagnetic Waves

  • EM waves are produced by accelerating charges and consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields.
  • They are transverse: E, B and the direction of propagation are mutually perpendicular.
  • They require no medium — can travel through vacuum, unlike sound.
  • All EM waves travel at speed c in vacuum, regardless of wavelength.

3 The Electromagnetic Spectrum

🖊️ The EM spectrum, arranged by increasing frequency (decreasing wavelength).
📐 Speed of EM waves
c = 1/√(μ0ε0) ≈ 3 × 10⁸ m/s = f λ
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the frequency of an EM wave of wavelength 600 nm (visible light).
  1. f = c/λ = 3×10⁸ / 600×10⁻⁹
🎯 Answer: f = 5×10¹⁴ Hz
Quick usesRadio: broadcasting · Microwave: radar, cooking · IR: remote controls, thermal imaging · Visible: seeing! · UV: sterilisation · X-ray: medical imaging · Gamma: cancer treatment, nuclear studies

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Displacement current (ε0 dΦE/dt) completes Ampere's law where conduction current is absent.
  • EM waves: transverse, self-sustaining oscillations of E and B, need no medium.
  • All EM waves travel at c in vacuum; they differ only in frequency/wavelength.
  • EM spectrum order (low→high frequency): radio, micro, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma.
