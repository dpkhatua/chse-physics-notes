Electric Charges and Fields

Two kinds of charge, one law, and an invisible field connecting every one of them.

1 Charge & Coulomb's Law

Charge is conserved (total charge of an isolated system never changes) and quantised (always an integer multiple of e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C). Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.

📐 Coulomb's law
F = k q1q2 / r² , k = 1/(4πε0) ≈ 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²

For multiple charges, the net force on any charge is the vector sum of forces due to each other charge individually — the superposition principle.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. Two point charges +2μC and +3μC are placed 30 cm apart. Find the force between them.
  1. F = kq1q2/r² = 9×10⁹ × 2×10⁻⁶ × 3×10⁻⁶ / (0.3)²
  2. = 9×10⁹ × 6×10⁻¹² / 0.09 = 54×10⁻³/0.09
🎯 Answer: F = 0.6 N (repulsive)
⚠️ Don't lose marks: Forgetting to convert μC to C, or cm to m, before substituting into Coulomb's law is the single most common numerical error in this chapter.

2 Electric Field & Dipoles

🖊️ Electric field lines radiate outward from a positive point charge.
📐 Electric field
E = F/q = kQ/r² (due to a point charge)
🖊️ An electric dipole: field lines run from the +ve to the −ve charge.
🧮 Derivation: Electric field on the equatorial line of a dipole
Given / Setup: A dipole of charges +q and −q separated by 2a; point P on the perpendicular bisector at distance r from the centre.
  1. Field due to +q and −q at P have equal magnitude kq/(r²+a²) each, directed along the lines joining charge to P.
  2. Components perpendicular to the dipole axis cancel by symmetry; components along the axis (anti-parallel to p) add.
  3. Each component along axis = [kq/(r²+a²)] × [a/√(r²+a²)]; total E = 2kqa/(r²+a²)^(3/2) = kp/(r²+a²)^(3/2)
  4. For r ≫ a: E ≈ kp/r³ (opposite direction to dipole moment p)
✅ Eequatorial = kp/(r²+a²)^(3/2) ≈ kp/r³ for r ≫ a
📐 Electric dipole
Dipole moment: p = q × 2a (from −q to +q)
Axial field: Eaxial = 2kpr/(r²−a²)² ≈ 2kp/r³
Torque in uniform field: τ = p × E

3 Electric Flux & Gauss's Law

🖊️ A Gaussian surface enclosing a charge — flux through it depends only on the enclosed charge.
📐 Gauss's law
Φ = ∮E·dA = qenclosed / ε0
🧮 Derivation: Electric field due to an infinite line charge (using Gauss's law)
Given / Setup: A uniformly charged infinite line with linear charge density λ; choose a cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius r and length L, coaxial with the line.
  1. By symmetry, E is radial and has the same magnitude at every point on the curved surface.
  2. Flux through flat circular ends = 0 (E is parallel to these surfaces); flux through curved surface = E × 2πrL
  3. By Gauss's law: E × 2πrL = λL/ε0
✅ E = λ/(2πε0r)

Gauss's law makes it easy to find E for highly symmetric charge distributions — infinite line charge, infinite sheet, and uniformly charged spherical shell — without doing messy integrals directly.

Handy resultsInfinite line charge: E = λ/2πε0r · Infinite sheet: E = σ/2ε0 · Charged spherical shell (outside): E = kQ/r² (behaves like a point charge!)

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Charge is conserved and quantised (q = ne).
  • Coulomb's law: F = kq1q2/r²; superposition gives net force from multiple charges.
  • Electric field E = F/q; dipole moment p = q(2a); torque τ = p×E.
  • Gauss's law: Φ = qenc/ε0 — powerful for symmetric charge distributions.
