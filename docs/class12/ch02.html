Electrostatic Potential and Capacitance

Energy per unit charge — and the clever devices built to store it.

1 Electric Potential

Electric potential (V) at a point = work done per unit charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to that point, without acceleration.

📐 Potential
V = kQ/r (point charge)
Potential energy of two charges: U = kq1q2/r

Equipotential surfaces are surfaces where potential is the same everywhere — no work is done moving a charge along one, and field lines are always perpendicular to them.

✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the potential at a point 20 cm from a point charge of 5μC.
  1. V = kQ/r = 9×10⁹ × 5×10⁻⁶ / 0.2
🎯 Answer: V = 2.25×10⁵ V

2 Capacitors & Capacitance

🖊️ A parallel plate capacitor stores equal and opposite charge on its two plates.
🧮 Derivation: Capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor
Given / Setup: Two parallel plates of area A separated by distance d in vacuum, with charge ±Q.
  1. Electric field between the plates (from Gauss's law for a conductor's surface): E = σ/ε0 = Q/(Aε0)
  2. Potential difference V = E × d = Qd/(Aε0)
  3. Capacitance C = Q/V = Q / [Qd/(Aε0)]
✅ C = ε0A/d
📐 Capacitance
C = Q/V
Parallel plate capacitor: C = ε0A/d (vacuum), C = Kε0A/d (with dielectric of constant K)
Energy stored: U = ½CV² = ½QV = Q²/2C
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A parallel plate capacitor has plate area 0.02 m² and separation 1 mm. Find its capacitance and the energy stored when charged to 200 V. (ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m)
  1. C = ε0A/d = 8.85×10⁻¹² × 0.02/0.001 = 1.77×10⁻¹⁰ F
  2. U = ½CV² = ½ × 1.77×10⁻¹⁰ × (200)² = ½ × 1.77×10⁻¹⁰ × 40000
🎯 Answer: C ≈ 177 pF, U ≈ 3.54×10⁻⁶ J

3 Combinations of Capacitors

📐 Series & parallel combination
Series: 1/Ceq = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + … (charge same on each, voltage adds)
Parallel: Ceq = C1 + C2 + … (voltage same on each, charge adds)
🧠 Yaad Rakho: Capacitors in series behave like resistors in parallel (and vice-versa) — the formulas literally swap roles!
💡 Score booster: When a question gives 'n identical capacitors of capacitance C', memorise: series → Ceq = C/n; parallel → Ceq = nC. This shortcut saves crucial time.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Potential V = work/charge; equipotential surfaces ⊥ field lines, no work done moving along them.
  • C = Q/V; parallel plate capacitor C = Kε0A/d.
  • Energy stored in a capacitor: U = ½CV².
  • Series capacitors: 1/Ceq adds; Parallel capacitors: Ceq adds directly.
