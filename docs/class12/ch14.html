Class XII · Unit IX · Chapter 14

Semiconductor Electronics: Materials, Devices and Simple Circuits

Silicon that's neither a good conductor nor a good insulator — and that's exactly what makes it useful.

1 Energy Bands in Solids

🖊️ Conductors, semiconductors and insulators differ mainly in the size of their energy gap.
  • Conductors: conduction and valence bands overlap — electrons move freely.
  • Insulators: a huge energy gap separates the bands — electrons can't jump across.
  • Semiconductors: a small energy gap — some electrons cross it at room temperature, more do as temperature rises (unlike metals!).

2 Intrinsic & Extrinsic Semiconductors

A pure (intrinsic) semiconductor like silicon has few free charge carriers. Doping it with impurities creates extrinsic semiconductors: p-type (trivalent dopant, creates 'holes' — majority carriers) and n-type (pentavalent dopant, creates extra free electrons — majority carriers).

⚠️ Don't lose marks: In p-type semiconductors, majority carriers are HOLES (not electrons) — a frequent mix-up when writing conduction-mechanism answers.

3 p-n Junction & Diode as a Rectifier

🖊️ At a p-n junction, a depletion region forms where mobile charge carriers have diffused away.

Joining p-type and n-type semiconductors forms a p-n junction diode. In forward bias (p to +ve, n to −ve), current flows easily; in reverse bias, only a tiny leakage current flows — this one-way behaviour makes a diode ideal as a rectifier, converting AC to DC.

Half-wave vs full-waveA single diode gives half-wave rectification (blocks one half of the AC cycle); four diodes in a bridge arrangement give full-wave rectification (uses both halves) — smoother DC output.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Conductors: bands overlap. Insulators: huge gap. Semiconductors: small gap, conductivity rises with temperature.
  • Doping creates p-type (holes, majority carriers) or n-type (electrons, majority carriers) semiconductors.
  • p-n junction diode conducts easily in forward bias, barely in reverse bias.
  • Diodes rectify AC to DC — single diode: half-wave; four-diode bridge: full-wave.

🎯 Exam Corner — practice in CHSE question style

Grouped the way CHSE papers weight questions. These are practice questions written in the board's style to test this chapter — not lifted from any specific year's paper.

1 mark
  1. What are majority charge carriers in an n-type semiconductor?
  2. Define depletion region in a p-n junction diode.
2 marks
  1. Distinguish between intrinsic and extrinsic semiconductors.
  2. Draw the circuit symbol of a p-n junction diode and label the p and n regions.
3 marks
  1. Explain the forward and reverse bias characteristics of a p-n junction diode.
5 marks
  1. Explain the working of a p-n junction diode as a full-wave rectifier with a labelled circuit diagram and input-output waveforms.
