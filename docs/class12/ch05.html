Class XII · Unit III · Chapter 5

Magnetism and Matter

Every magnet is secretly just current loops, arranged neatly.

1 Bar Magnet as an Equivalent Solenoid

🖊️ A bar magnet behaves like a solenoid — field lines emerge from N, curve around, and enter S.

A bar magnet's field pattern outside closely matches that of a finite solenoid carrying current — this connects 'ordinary' magnets to the same physics as current loops.

📐 Magnetic dipole (bar magnet)
Magnetic dipole moment: m = qm × 2l
Torque in uniform field: τ = m × B
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A bar magnet of dipole moment 2 A·m² is placed in a uniform field of 0.5 T at 30° to the field. Find the torque on it.
  1. τ = mB sinθ = 2 × 0.5 × sin30° = 1 × 0.5
🎯 Answer: τ = 0.5 N·m

2 Classifying Magnetic Materials

  • Diamagnetic: weakly repelled by a magnetic field; no permanent dipole moment (e.g. bismuth, copper).
  • Paramagnetic: weakly attracted; have permanent atomic dipoles that partially align with the field (e.g. aluminium, sodium).
  • Ferromagnetic: strongly attracted; dipoles align in large domains, retaining magnetisation even after the field is removed (e.g. iron, cobalt, nickel).
Effect of temperatureAbove a critical temperature (Curie temperature), ferromagnetic materials lose their strong magnetism and start behaving like paramagnetic materials — thermal agitation disrupts the aligned domains.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • A bar magnet's external field pattern resembles a finite current-carrying solenoid.
  • Magnetic dipole moment m = qm(2l); torque τ = m×B.
  • Diamagnetic: weakly repelled. Paramagnetic: weakly attracted. Ferromagnetic: strongly attracted, retains magnetism.
  • Above the Curie temperature, ferromagnets become paramagnetic.
