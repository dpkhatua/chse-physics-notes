Class XII · Unit VIII · Chapter 13

Nuclei

Split it or fuse it — either way, a tiny bit of mass turns into a huge amount of energy.

1 Nuclear Composition & Size

A nucleus contains protons and neutrons (nucleons), held together at extremely short range by the strong nuclear force — strong enough to overcome the electrostatic repulsion between protons packed so closely together.

📐 Nuclear size
R = R0 A^(1/3) , R0 ≈ 1.2 fm
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the radius of a nucleus with mass number 64. (R0 = 1.2 fm)
  1. R = R0 A^(1/3) = 1.2 × 64^(1/3) = 1.2 × 4
🎯 Answer: R = 4.8 fm

2 Mass Defect & Binding Energy

🖊️ Binding energy per nucleon peaks around iron (A ≈ 56) — the most stable nuclei.
📐 Mass-energy relation
E = mc²
Mass defect: Δm = (Zmp + Nmn) − Mnucleus
Binding energy: BE = Δm c²
✏️ Solved Example
Q. Find the binding energy of a helium nucleus (mass defect Δm = 0.0304 u). (1u = 931 MeV/c²)
  1. BE = Δm × 931 MeV = 0.0304 × 931
🎯 Answer: BE ≈ 28.3 MeV

The binding energy per nucleon curve explains both fission and fusion: nuclei can release energy by moving toward the peak (around iron) — heavy nuclei splitting (fission) or light nuclei combining (fusion) both increase average binding energy per nucleon.

3 Nuclear Fission & Fusion

🖊️ A neutron striking a heavy nucleus like U-235 splits it, releasing more neutrons and energy.
  • Nuclear fission: a heavy nucleus (e.g. U-235) splits into lighter nuclei after absorbing a neutron, releasing huge energy plus more neutrons — enabling a chain reaction (used in nuclear reactors, atom bombs).
  • Nuclear fusion: light nuclei (e.g. hydrogen isotopes) combine into a heavier nucleus at extremely high temperature/pressure, releasing even more energy per unit mass — the process powering the Sun.
💡 Score booster: For 'explain fission vs fusion' questions, always mention the binding-energy-per-nucleon curve as the underlying reason energy is released — this single diagram/explanation answers most conceptual questions in this chapter.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • Nucleus = protons + neutrons, held by the strong nuclear force; R = R0A^(1/3).
  • Mass defect Δm converts to binding energy via E = mc².
  • Binding energy per nucleon peaks near iron — driving both fission (heavy→lighter) and fusion (light→heavier).
  • Fission powers nuclear reactors; fusion powers the Sun.
