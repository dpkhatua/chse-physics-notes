Ohm's law, resistors, and the two rules that solve any circuit ever drawn.

1 Current, Drift Velocity & Ohm's Law

Electric current is the rate of flow of charge: I = q/t. Inside a conductor, free electrons don't travel in straight lines at high speed — they drift slowly (drift velocity vd) superimposed on their random thermal motion, under the push of an electric field.

🖊️ A simple circuit: a cell drives current I through a resistor R.
🧮 Derivation: Relation between current and drift velocity
Given / Setup: Consider a conductor of length L, area A, with n free electrons per unit volume, each moving with drift velocity vd.
  1. In time t, each electron moves a distance vdt, so all electrons within a volume A×(vdt) cross a given cross-section.
  2. Number of electrons crossing = n × A × vd × t
  3. Total charge crossing q = n A vd t e, so current I = q/t = nAvde
✅ I = nAvde
📐 Ohm's law & resistivity
V = IR (Ohm's law, for ohmic conductors)
R = ρL/A (ρ = resistivity, a material property)
I = neAvd (n = free electron density)
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A wire of resistivity 1.7×10⁻⁸ Ωm, length 2 m and area 1 mm² carries a current of 2 A from a 0.068 V source. Verify Ohm's law by finding R two ways.
  1. From geometry: R = ρL/A = 1.7×10⁻⁸ × 2 / 1×10⁻⁶ = 0.034 Ω
  2. From Ohm's law: R = V/I = 0.068/2 = 0.034 Ω — both match!
🎯 Answer: R = 0.034 Ω

Resistance generally increases with temperature for metals (more collisions), but decreases for semiconductors (more charge carriers get freed as temperature rises).

2 Cells, EMF & Internal Resistance

📐 Cell equations
EMF ε = V + Ir (r = internal resistance)
Terminal voltage: V = ε − Ir
  • Cells in series: EMFs add, useful for higher voltage.
  • Cells in parallel: useful for higher current, EMF stays same as one cell (if identical cells).
✏️ Solved Example
Q. A cell of EMF 2V and internal resistance 0.5Ω is connected to an external resistance of 4.5Ω. Find the current and terminal voltage.
  1. I = ε/(R+r) = 2/(4.5+0.5) = 2/5 = 0.4 A
  2. V = ε − Ir = 2 − 0.4×0.5 = 2 − 0.2 = 1.8 V
🎯 Answer: I = 0.4 A, V = 1.8 V

3 Kirchhoff's Rules & Wheatstone Bridge

📐 Kirchhoff's rules
Junction rule (charge conservation): ΣIin = ΣIout at any junction
Loop rule (energy conservation): ΣV = 0 around any closed loop

The Wheatstone bridge is a clever four-resistor circuit used to measure an unknown resistance precisely — when the bridge is 'balanced' (no current through the galvanometer), P/Q = R/S.

⚠️ Don't lose marks: In Kirchhoff's loop rule, sign convention errors (treating a voltage rise as a drop or vice versa while tracing the loop) is the top reason for wrong answers — always fix a direction and stick to it.

⚡ Quick Recap

  • I = q/t; V = IR for ohmic conductors; R = ρL/A.
  • Drift velocity is the slow, net electron motion caused by the applied field.
  • EMF = V + Ir; terminal voltage drops as current drawn increases.
  • Kirchhoff's junction rule (charge conservation) + loop rule (energy conservation) solve any circuit.
